Chapter 01

What is LLM Evaluation?

Before you can improve a language model system, you need a reliable way to measure whether it's actually good. That's what evaluation is for.

Simple Explanation

LLM evaluation is the process of systematically checking whether a language model's outputs are correct, safe, helpful, and reliable — the same way a teacher grades an exam, except the "student" is an AI system and the "exam" runs millions of times a day in production.

Technical Explanation

Formally, evaluation is a function E(input, output, reference?, context?) → score(s) that maps a model's behavior on a given input to one or more quantitative or qualitative judgments. These scores can be automatic (string/statistical metrics), model-based (another LLM judges the output), or human (expert or crowd raters). Evaluation happens offline (before deployment, on curated datasets) and online (continuously, on live production traffic).

Real-world Analogy

Think of an airline pilot. Before ever flying passengers, they pass rigorous simulator tests (offline evaluation on benchmarks). Once flying, black-box flight recorders and live telemetry continuously monitor performance (online/production evaluation). Both matter — the AI system equivalent is the same.

🔍Why Evaluation Exists

LLMs are non-deterministic, trained on internet-scale data, and can fail silently — producing fluent, confident, and completely wrong answers. Unlike traditional software where a bug throws an exception, LLM failures often look correct. Evaluation is the only way to catch this before your users do.

Interview Tip

A common interview question: "How is evaluating an LLM different from evaluating traditional software?" Good answer: traditional software has deterministic pass/fail unit tests; LLMs require probabilistic, multi-dimensional, often subjective scoring against reference-free or reference-based criteria, and must be re-evaluated continuously because model behavior drifts with prompt, data, and version changes.

Chapter 02

Why Evaluation Matters

Skipping evaluation doesn't remove risk — it just makes the risk invisible until it becomes a headline.

❌ Without Evaluation

  • Hallucinations ship to real users unnoticed
  • Bias and unsafe outputs go undetected
  • Regressions from prompt/model changes are invisible
  • Cost and latency spiral out of control
  • No data-driven way to compare models or prompts

✅ With Evaluation

  • Failures caught before or immediately after launch
  • Safety issues flagged and mitigated systematically
  • Regressions caught automatically in CI
  • Cost/latency tracked against SLOs
  • Objective, reproducible model/prompt comparisons

🔢Metric Deep Dive: Faithfulness

Definition: the proportion of claims in a generated answer that are directly supported by the provided source/context.

faithfulness.py
def faithfulness_score(claims, context):
    # claims: list of atomic statements extracted from the answer
    supported = [c for c in claims if is_supported(c, context)]
    return len(supported) / len(claims)  # range: 0.0 - 1.0

Advantages: catches hallucination directly relative to source truth. Disadvantages: requires claim decomposition (often via another LLM), sensitive to how "supported" is defined. Production Usage: gates RAG answers below a faithfulness threshold from being shown; core metric in Ragas, TruLens, and DeepEval.

Common Mistake

Chasing leaderboard rank on public benchmarks without validating against your own domain data. A model that tops MMLU may still perform poorly on your specific support tickets or legal documents.

📕Building Your Own Golden Dataset

  1. Sample real production queries across difficulty tiers (easy/medium/hard/edge case)
  2. Include known failure modes discovered in monitoring
  3. Write or verify reference answers with domain experts
  4. Version the dataset — treat it like code
  5. Re-balance periodically as usage patterns shift
Interview Tip

"Why can't you rely solely on public benchmarks for a production system?" — Because benchmarks measure general capability, not domain fit, and many are partially leaked into training data (contamination), inflating scores.

Chapter 07

Human Evaluation

Humans remain the gold standard for nuanced judgment — expensive, slow, but irreplaceable for calibrating everything else.

⚖️

Pairwise Comparison

Show two outputs, ask which is better — more reliable than absolute scoring.

📊

Likert Scale

Rate a response 1-5 on a specific dimension (e.g. helpfulness).

🏆

Preference Ranking

Order multiple candidates from best to worst.

✍️

Annotation

Labeling spans, categories, or errors within a response.

📜

Rubrics

Structured criteria checklists raters apply consistently.

🎓

Expert Review

Domain specialists (doctors, lawyers) validate high-stakes outputs.

👥

Crowdsourcing

Distributed raters (e.g. Mechanical Turk) for scale, with quality controls.

🤝

Agreement Metrics

Cohen's/Fleiss' Kappa measure rater consistency and rubric quality.

🔢Inter-Annotator Agreement

Cohen's Kappa formula:

kappa.txt
κ = (P_observed − P_expected) / (1 − P_expected)

Where P_observed is the raw agreement rate between two raters and P_expected is the agreement expected purely by chance. Kappa > 0.8 is considered strong agreement; below 0.4 suggests your rubric is ambiguous.

Common Mistake

Using absolute 1-10 scores without anchoring examples. Raters drift wildly without calibration examples — pairwise comparison or rubric-anchored scales are far more reliable.

Production Tip

Use human evaluation to build and calibrate your LLM-as-a-Judge prompts — human labels become the "ground truth" the judge model is validated against.

Chapter 08

Automatic Evaluation Metrics

Fast, cheap, reproducible — but each metric only sees a narrow slice of "quality."

BLEU

n-gram▾

Formula: BLEU = BP × exp(∑ w_n log p_n) — geometric mean of n-gram precision, with a brevity penalty (BP) for short outputs.

Example: candidate "the cat sat on the mat" vs reference "the cat is on the mat" scores moderately due to overlapping bigrams.

Advantages: fast, standard in MT. Disadvantages: penalizes valid paraphrases; poor correlation with human judgment for open-ended generation.

ROUGE

n-gram recall▾

Formula: ROUGE-N = overlapping n-grams / total n-grams in reference. ROUGE-L uses longest common subsequence.

Production Usage: summarization quality (recall-oriented — did the summary cover the reference content?).

METEOR

n-gram + synonym▾

Combines precision/recall with stemming and synonym matching, correlating better with human judgment than BLEU for translation tasks.

CIDEr

image captioning▾

TF-IDF weighted n-gram consensus across multiple human reference captions — designed specifically for image captioning quality.

BERTScore

embedding-based▾

Uses contextual BERT embeddings to compute token-level cosine similarity between candidate and reference — captures paraphrase/semantic similarity that BLEU/ROUGE miss.

MoverScore

embedding-based▾

Uses Earth Mover's Distance over contextual embeddings for a soft, semantically-aware alignment score.

BLEURT / COMET

learned metrics▾

Fine-tuned neural models trained on human judgment data to predict a quality score directly — higher correlation with humans than rule-based metrics, at the cost of needing a trained model.

Exact Match / F1 / Precision / Recall

classification▾

Precision = TP / (TP + FP), Recall = TP / (TP + FN), F1 = 2PR / (P + R). Widely used for QA span matching and classification-style tasks.

Confusion Matrix, ROC, AUC

classification▾

Used when framing evaluation as classification (e.g. hallucination detector, safety classifier). AUC summarizes the trade-off between true-positive and false-positive rate across thresholds.

LLM-as-a-Judge

Use a strong LLM to grade another LLM's outputs — fast, scalable, and surprisingly well correlated with human raters when designed carefully.

🎯Concept & Architecture

⚠️Judge Biases & Limitations

🧠

Self-Preference Bias

A model tends to rate its own outputs (or outputs from its "family") more favorably.

📌

Position Bias

In pairwise comparisons, judges often favor whichever answer appears first (or last).

🔒

Prompt Leakage

The judge's own rubric or reasoning can leak into the visible verdict, or be gamed by prompt injection in the candidate response.

📊

Judge Calibration

Judge scores must be validated against human labels periodically — calibration drifts as models update.

👥

Judge Ensemble

Using multiple judge models/prompts and aggregating reduces variance and single-model bias.

🚫

Limitations

Judges can be fooled by fluent-but-wrong text, and inherit the blind spots of the underlying model.

Production Tip

Randomize answer order in pairwise judging to cancel out position bias, and periodically sample judge verdicts for human spot-checks — treat the judge itself as a monitored system, not ground truth.

Interview Tip

"How do you validate an LLM judge?" — Compute agreement (e.g. Cohen's Kappa) between judge verdicts and a human-labeled gold set; iterate on the rubric/prompt until agreement is acceptable (commonly >0.7-0.8).

🔧

💾

🤕

🛠Trajectory Evaluation

Rather than scoring only the final output, trajectory evaluation scores the full sequence of (thought, action, observation) steps — catching cases where an agent "got lucky" via a wrong path, or failed efficiently vs. inefficiently.

Step 1 — Thought

"I need to find the current weather in Tokyo."

Step 2 — Action

Call get_weather(city="Tokyo")

Step 3 — Observation

Tool returns: 18°C, light rain.

Step 4 — Reflection

"This answers the user's question directly."

Step 5 — Final Answer

"It's currently 18°C with light rain in Tokyo."

Important

Task Completion Rate alone hides inefficiency. Two agents both hitting 90% completion can differ hugely in average steps, retries, and cost per task — always pair completion rate with efficiency metrics.

🎯Pass@k Formula

pass_at_k.txt
pass@k = E[1 − C(n−c, k) / C(n, k)]

Where n is total samples generated, c is the number that pass, and k is the number you'd actually try in practice. This unbiased estimator avoids simply generating more samples to inflate scores.

📜Benchmarks

HumanEvalMBPPSWE-benchLiveCodeBench

HumanEval and MBPP test isolated function-level correctness. SWE-bench and LiveCodeBench push toward real-world, repository-scale software engineering tasks — much closer to production coding agent use cases.

Production Tip

For coding assistants, combine execution-based metrics (pass@k, tests) with static analysis (security, lint) — a model can pass tests while introducing subtle vulnerabilities.

Chapter 17

Hallucination Evaluation

The single most requested — and hardest to fully solve — evaluation problem in production LLM systems.

Intrinsic Hallucination

Output contradicts the given source/context directly — e.g. context says "1889" but the model says "1901."

Extrinsic Hallucination

Output adds information that cannot be verified from the source at all — plausible-sounding but unconfirmable claims.

🔍Detection Methods

  • Grounding checks: verify each claim against retrieved/source text
  • Citation checking: confirm cited sources actually support the cited claim
  • Knowledge verification: cross-reference against a trusted knowledge base
  • Fact checking: decompose into atomic claims, verify each independently
  • Self-consistency: sample multiple generations, flag divergence as a hallucination signal
  • NLI-based entailment: use a natural-language-inference model to check claim-vs-source entailment

💫 Hallucination Detector Demo Live Tool

Paste a source context and a claim. This heuristic demo flags unsupported claims via keyword-overlap grounding (illustrative only).

Common Mistake

Treating "the model didn't cite a source" as automatically hallucinated, and "it cited a source" as automatically grounded. Citations can be fabricated too — always verify the citation actually supports the claim.

Interview Tip

"How would you build a hallucination detector at scale?" — Decompose responses into atomic claims (via an LLM), check each against retrieved context using an NLI/entailment model, aggregate into a faithfulness score, and periodically calibrate thresholds against human-labeled data.

Chapter 22

Case Studies

Lessons from how real products approach evaluation at scale.

ChatGPT / Consumer Assistants

Conversational▾

Strategy: combination of RLHF preference modeling, large-scale human preference collection, and automated regression suites across model versions.

Challenges: balancing helpfulness vs. safety refusals; measuring subjective "likeability" at massive scale.

Lesson: preference-based pairwise human evaluation scales better than absolute scoring for subjective conversational quality.

GitHub Copilot / Coding Assistants

Code▾

Strategy: acceptance-rate telemetry (did the developer keep the suggestion?), combined with offline pass@k on benchmark suites.

Challenges: acceptance ≠ correctness — a developer might accept then immediately delete a suggestion.

Lesson: pair implicit production signals with periodic explicit correctness sampling.

Perplexity / Search-Grounded Answers

RAG▾

Strategy: heavy emphasis on citation accuracy and groundedness metrics since the core value prop is trustworthy sourcing.

Lesson: when the product promise is "cited answers," citation-accuracy evaluation becomes a top-line business metric, not just an engineering one.

Cursor / Windsurf (AI IDEs)

Agents▾

Strategy: trajectory-level evaluation on real repository tasks (similar to SWE-bench style), plus extensive dogfooding/telemetry.

Lesson: for agentic coding tools, end-to-end task success across multi-file changes matters more than single-completion accuracy.

Enterprise RAG (Healthcare / Finance / Legal)

High-stakes▾

Strategy: mandatory human expert review for high-risk outputs, strict groundedness/citation thresholds, audit trails for every generated answer.

Challenges: regulatory compliance, zero tolerance for certain hallucination types (e.g. dosage errors, incorrect legal citations).

Lesson: in regulated domains, evaluation thresholds are often compliance requirements, not just quality targets.

Chapter 24

Interview Questions

40+ questions spanning beginner to architect level. Click any question to reveal the answer.

What is LLM evaluation?

▾
The process of measuring the quality, safety, and reliability of a language model's outputs using automatic metrics, LLM judges, or human review.

What's the difference between offline and online evaluation?

▾
Offline runs on curated datasets before deployment; online runs continuously on live production traffic after deployment.

What is a hallucination?

▾
A confidently stated output that is factually incorrect or unsupported by the given source/context.

Name three automatic evaluation metrics.

▾
BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore (also acceptable: F1, Exact Match, METEOR).

What is LLM-as-a-Judge?

▾
Using a (typically stronger) LLM to score or compare the outputs of another model against a rubric.

What does "faithfulness" measure in RAG?

▾
Whether claims in the generated answer are actually supported by the retrieved context.

What is pass@k?

▾
The probability that at least one of k sampled code generations passes all unit tests.

Why can't you rely on BLEU for open-ended chat evaluation?

▾
BLEU assumes one "correct" phrasing and penalizes valid paraphrases, correlating poorly with human judgment on open-ended generation.

How do you evaluate a RAG pipeline end-to-end?

▾
Evaluate retriever (precision@k, recall@k, MRR, NDCG) and generator (faithfulness, answer relevancy) separately, then measure end-to-end groundedness and context recall/precision.

What is position bias in LLM judges, and how do you mitigate it?

▾
Judges tend to favor whichever candidate appears first/last in pairwise comparisons; mitigate by randomizing or swapping order and averaging results.

How do you measure inter-annotator agreement, and why does it matter?

▾
Cohen's/Fleiss' Kappa; low agreement signals an ambiguous rubric that needs clarification before scaling human evaluation.

Explain the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic hallucination.

▾
Intrinsic contradicts the given source directly; extrinsic adds unverifiable information not present in the source at all.

What is shadow deployment and when would you use it?

▾
Running a new model/version in parallel on live traffic without serving its output to users, to compare against the current version risk-free.

What's the difference between context precision and context recall?

▾
Precision measures how much of the retrieved context is relevant; recall measures how much of the needed relevant information was actually retrieved.

How would you evaluate a multi-turn conversational agent's memory?

▾
Use "memory probe" tests: plant facts early in a conversation and query them many turns later, measuring recall accuracy.

What's the difference between toxicity and bias evaluation?

▾
Toxicity measures presence of harmful/abusive language; bias measures systematic skew in treatment/quality across different groups or viewpoints.

How do you validate that an LLM judge is trustworthy?

▾
Compute agreement between judge verdicts and human-labeled gold data (e.g. Cohen's Kappa), iterate the rubric until agreement is acceptable, and re-validate periodically as models update.

Design an evaluation strategy for an agentic system with tool calling.

▾
Evaluate trajectory (planning, reasoning), tool selection/parameter accuracy, execution success, recovery from failures, and overall task completion — not just the final answer.

How would you detect embedding drift in production?

▾
Continuously compare the distribution of query/document embeddings against a stored baseline window using statistical distance measures (e.g. population stability index, KL divergence).

How do you build an unbiased pass@k estimator?

▾
Use the combinatorial estimator 1 − C(n−c,k)/C(n,k) rather than naively sampling k times, which is a biased and higher-variance estimate.

How would you evaluate calibration in an LLM's confidence?

▾
Bucket predictions by stated confidence, compare against actual accuracy in each bucket (reliability diagram), and compute Expected Calibration Error (ECE).

What are the tradeoffs of canary vs. shadow deployment for evaluation?

▾
Canary exposes real users to limited risk but gets direct behavioral signal; shadow has zero user risk but can't measure real user reaction/engagement, only output comparison.

Your RAG chatbot's faithfulness score drops 15% after a vector DB migration. How do you debug it?

▾
Isolate retriever vs generator: check retrieval precision/recall pre/post migration, verify embedding model consistency, inspect chunk boundaries, and compare a fixed eval set before/after the migration in isolation.

Users report your assistant "forgets" things mentioned 20 messages ago. How would you evaluate and fix this?

▾
Run memory-probe evaluations at increasing turn distances to find the effective retention limit, then consider summarization/compression strategies or persistent memory retrieval.

A judge model consistently prefers longer answers regardless of quality. How do you fix your eval pipeline?

▾
This is length bias — explicitly instruct the judge to penalize unnecessary verbosity, normalize for length in the rubric, or add a conciseness criterion as a separate scored dimension.

Design a company-wide LLM evaluation platform serving 20 product teams.

▾
Central shared tracing/eval infrastructure with pluggable per-team rubrics, a shared judge-calibration service, standard metric libraries, and self-serve dataset/regression suite tooling — balancing central governance with team-level flexibility.

How do you enforce evaluation gates in CI/CD for a high-velocity prompt-engineering team?

▾
Every prompt/model change triggers an automated run against a golden regression set with defined pass thresholds; failures block merge; a small canary rollout with automatic rollback covers residual production risk.

How would you build a compliant evaluation system for a regulated healthcare LLM product?

▾
Mandatory human clinical review for high-risk outputs, immutable audit trails linking every generated answer to its evaluation trace, strict groundedness thresholds, and documented model-change validation procedures tied to regulatory sign-off.

The Future of Evaluation

Evaluation itself is becoming automated, agentic, and self-improving.

Agentic AI Evaluation

Standardized trajectory-level benchmarks for long-horizon autonomous agents.

Self-Evaluation

Models critiquing and correcting their own outputs before responding.

Auto Evals

Automatic generation of evaluation datasets and rubrics from production logs.

Synthetic Data

LLM-generated edge cases expanding eval coverage beyond human-authored sets.

Continuous Evaluation

Evaluation infrastructure fully embedded as an always-on production service.

Judge Models

Purpose-trained, specialized judge models replacing general-purpose LLM judges.

World Models

Simulated environments for evaluating agents against realistic dynamics.

Autonomous QA

End-to-end automated quality assurance pipelines requiring minimal human oversight.

Pro Tip

Stay close to research trends, but don't wait for "perfect" tooling — the fundamentals (multiple metrics, human calibration, continuous monitoring) remain valuable regardless of how the tooling landscape evolves.
